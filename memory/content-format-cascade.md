# Repurposing rule: video is always the base format

**A short video (a Reel or a Short) is always the base format and the starting point**, never text. From the finished video, narrow the idea into the other formats (a thread, an Instagram carousel, a long post).

**Why:** one short video feeds several platforms at once. The same file can publish as a Reel and as a short video elsewhere, with no re-edit. That maximizes reach for the least production effort at the start. Deriving text from a video that is already recorded is cheap: reuse the lines and the script. The reverse, expanding a text into a video later, means recording from scratch, which is the most expensive step in the pipeline. It is the only step that requires the creator in person, and it is still manual.

This is a system-wide repurposing rule, not a rule for one series. It applies to any new idea that could become a video.

**How to apply:** when planning how a new idea becomes content, always ask first whether it can be a short video, before going straight to a text format. If it can, the video is the base piece. It enters the record list with a hook, value, and call-to-action script. Text formats in ready content are repurposed from it, not written in parallel. Over time this should show up in the mix of the editorial calendar.

**Known trap:** a repurposed piece, such as a carousel, is sometimes logged as in progress, with the copy marked ready, before the base video is even recorded. Check that when reviewing ready content. Always update the text piece after the real video is recorded if the final script diverges from the earlier draft.

## Complement: mark the atoms before deriving

Before slicing the base video into a carousel, a thread, or a caption, mark the **atoms** in the script: hook, promise, proof (a number, a screenshot, a result), objection (what stops the audience), mechanism (how it works), and call to action. Each derivative comes from one atom and needs **its own angle**. If it has no reason to exist beyond leftover material, it does not ship. Do not publish two competing derivatives on the same channel on the same day, and spread them across the week. Do not drop them all at once. Match the derivative to the goal: teaching becomes a carousel or a thread, proof becomes an annotated screenshot or a before-and-after, and retention becomes a checklist or a recap.
