# Substack best practices

Researched from web search plus `last30days`. **Platform and algorithm notes go stale fast.** Search again if this note is older than about three months. It complements the launch plan, if one exists, and `content-format-cascade.md` (the long post is the last step of repurposing).

## What Substack is, and what it is not

- **It is not a top-of-funnel growth channel** the way a Reel or a short video is. It is a layer for (1) a portable audience (the email list), (2) retention and relationship, (3) long-form search, because the platform already has authority in web search, and (4) monetization later.
- Even so, **about half of new subscriptions now start inside the platform** (Notes, Recommendations, and discovery). There is an internal growth engine. It is slow, and it depends on taking part in the network, not only on publishing.
- Recommendations between publications drive about 60% of new-subscriber growth for accounts that grow. It is the strongest lever on the platform.

## Discovery — how distribution works

- **Notes is the engine for a new account.** The Notes feed no longer mostly shows people the reader already follows. Most of what appears comes from people the reader has never followed. It is algorithmic discovery. The stated goal is "discover, subscribe, and ideally pay", not time in the app.
- **Restack is the key metric.** When someone restacks a Note, the platform amplifies it to that person's audience. It is the fastest path to new, relevant readers. Restacking generously inside the niche earns algorithmic favor back.
- **A Note with a photo or a video wins.** About one in three Notes already has media. A text-only Note in the style of a short post barely moves.
- **A story beats a loose tip.** The algorithm favors a narrative Note over "three quick tips".
- **A 10-5-1 routine for new accounts:** each day, like about 10 Notes in the niche, leave a real comment on about 5, and start 1 real conversation. Also find 3 to 10 publications at a similar stage and build a real relationship with 2 or 3 of them. That is the base for swapping Recommendations.
- **Search:** connect a custom domain (about US$50, once). Backlinks then point at that domain, and the property can be registered in a search console. The platform alone does not index cleanly. Set an **SEO title separately** from the email title: a curious title for the inbox, a keyword title for search, on the same post.
- **Live:** going live sends an automatic email to the whole list and skips the feed. Newer live and video surfaces are what the platform is promoting.

## Post craft (from an analysis of about 94,000 posts)

- **The title is the email subject.** What is typed in the composer is the exact text that lands in the inbox.
- **Title length: 13 to 17 words** performs better (about 27% above titles of 1 to 5 words). About 43% of posts use a title that is too short.
- **First person ("I", "my") in the title: about 30% better.** Only about 5.5% of posts use it. It is the most underused pattern, and it draws the most comments.
- **A negative frame ("never", "wrong", "nobody"): about a 60% lift.** A contraction such as "isn't" helps too. Use it sparingly, and only when it is true.
- **A number at the front of the title: about a 47% lift.** Used in only about 1.6% of posts.
- **A question in the title: about 7% worse.** Exception: an ellipsis ("...") does well.
- **Do not summarize in the title. Open a gap.** The title has to make a claim the reader cannot judge without clicking.
- **Subtitle: 6 to 10 words**, extending the title's promise (not restating it). A subtitle of 1 to 5 words makes no difference.
- **A cover image is mandatory** (about an 81% performance gap; about 92.5% of posts already use one). No cover is a real penalty.
- **Body length:** 2,000 or more words draws more reaction in absolute terms, but 200 to 499 words returns about three times more reaction per word. **Sustainable working zone: 1,000 to 2,000 words.**
- **The day of the week does not matter** (the difference is negligible). Consistency does.

## Monetization — when to turn on paid

- **Free-to-paid conversion, typical case:** the median newsletter converts **about 0.62%** of free subscribers. The top quartile is 2 to 5%. The "5 to 10%" figure the platform popularized describes the **best performers**, not the typical one.
- **A specialized niche converts higher.** Some categories reach about 8%.
- **Plan revenue at 1 to 2%**, not at 10%.
- **Build an engaged free list BEFORE turning paid on.** Accounts that gate early, before they have an engaged audience, consistently lose to accounts that waited.
- **What sells on the paid tier is a high-utility asset, not more essays.** A template library, access to a tool, a step-by-step playbook, a live roundtable. People pay to save time, not to read more commentary.
- The platform keeps 10% plus the card processor fee (on the order of 2.9% plus a small fixed fee). Confirm the processor works in the creator's country.

## Repurposing a video into a post (fits the cascade)

- Paste the video URL on an empty line in the editor. The platform renders the embedded player on its own.
- **Do not dump a raw transcript.** Take the transcript, cut filler, extract the 5 to 7 most useful insights, and build the post around them: open with the best moment of the video, then the lessons in order of usefulness. It is a curated distillation, not a dump.
- Always keep captions or a transcript on the embedded video (access and search).

## Gotchas

- **The platform runs an AI-text detector (Pangram) on posts** and surfaces the result. Authors complain about it often. Write in the creator's own voice (see the voice guide under `memory/`). Never paste raw generated text.
- New "media startup" accounts have reported a fast ban in the first month, across this platform and others. Do not automate publishing aggressively. Do not look like a spam account.
- "Publish and forget" does not work. Reach comes from the creator's own interaction (replying to comments, restacking, Notes) in the first hours.
- Text-only Notes are dead. Always include media.

## How to apply

- Start **free**, on a weekly or biweekly cadence, and keep it. Do not assume two languages unless the creator has decided that.
- Work Notes from day one (the 10-5-1 routine). It is the only real organic growth on the platform. Restack accounts in the same niche.
- Seek Recommendations with 3 to 5 newsletters in the same niche and at a similar stage.
- Connect a custom domain early if search is a goal (about US$50, once).
- Post titles: first person, specific, with a gap, 13 to 17 words, no question mark. Always a cover. A separate SEO title that carries the keyword.
- Turn on a paid tier only when the cadence is proven, the free list is engaged, and a utility asset is ready (a template, a checklist, or a packaged walkthrough).
