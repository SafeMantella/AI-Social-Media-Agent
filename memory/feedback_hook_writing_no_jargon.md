# Hook writing rule: no unexplained jargon

A hook (the first 3 seconds of a Reel, or slide 1 of a carousel) must not use a specialist term that the piece only explains later. Replace it with a universal consequence anyone understands immediately, even without the term. The specialist term can, and should, appear from the second block or slide, once the piece has promised to explain why.

**Why:** a hook that assumes the term loses people at the exact moment retention matters most. Watch time in the first 3 seconds is the top ranking factor on short-form platforms. See `project_social_media_status.md`.

**How to apply:** when writing a hook for any new piece (Reel, Short, or carousel), check whether the hook depends on a specialist term that is only explained later. If it does, rewrite around the universal pain or urgency first, and introduce the jargon in the next block. A one-off decision to drop the call to action while the goal is pure growth or retention does not extend to the whole catalog. Confirm that with the creator, piece by piece.

**Confirmed exception:** the rule is for non-obvious specialist jargon, not for ordinary words. If the word is common outside the niche, it does not have to wait for the second block. Hold back only terms that are truly specific to the niche. A straight, informative hook can name an everyday word immediately when (a) the word is ordinary language, not niche jargon, and (b) the concept itself is the hook, with the stakes already inside the sentence.

**A published trial qualifies that exception:** a video whose hook used an ordinary word, and so did not break the jargon rule, still held almost no non-followers through to the end. The hook had two separate structural problems, confirmed by outside research (see `growth_sales_critical_lens.md`): (1) it was **declarative / definitional** — it resolved its own thesis in the same sentence, with no curiosity gap and no specific pain; (2) it had **two clauses (about 19 words)** — too heavy for the real retention window. About half the audience is gone in the first 3 seconds, and on-screen text has to be read almost instantly by people watching with the sound off. **The exception "an ordinary word may appear in the hook" still stands, but it is not enough on its own. The hook also has to lead with specific pain, a result, or curiosity (one of the four families in `growth_sales_critical_lens.md`) and fit in one short sentence, not a definition of a concept.** For cold pieces, the ones that must reach non-followers, prefer a universal pain that does not require already knowing the tool. The tool or the specialist term enters in the next block.
