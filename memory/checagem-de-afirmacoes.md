# Claims check

Every factual claim in a script, caption, or on-screen text is checked before the creator approves the piece, and again on the creator's final text. A creator's edit can bring an error back.

## When

1. After the script is written and before it is shown for approval (step 3 of `memory/feedback-notion-canonical-roteiro-hook.md`).
2. On the final text after the creator edits, before the pre-publish review.

## How

List every claim that states a fact: a number, a date, a cause, a result, a place, a diagnosis, a title, a quote, or a "first / only / biggest". Check each with a web search or a source the creator supplied. Do not check opinions. Write the table in the same page or file as the script, under "Claims check", dated:

| claim | verdict | source | safer wording |
|---|---|---|---|

Verdict is one of `confirmed`, `overstated`, `wrong`, or `not found`. `not found` means: say so, and either cut the claim or mark it as unverified. Never fill a gap from memory.

## Rules

- A cause that the source does not state is `overstated`. "The film changed the law" needs a source that says the film did. Often the safer wording is the verifiable effect.
- Check the exact word. "Staged" and "fake" are different claims, and so are a diagnosis and a label.
- Check places and attributions, including in lines the creator wrote.
- Show the creator the table and the safer wording. The creator decides. Keep their stronger wording if they choose it, and say the risk once.
- Put the original title next to a localized title on screen when the localized title is not confirmed.
- If the speech and the caption say different things about the same fact, flag it.
