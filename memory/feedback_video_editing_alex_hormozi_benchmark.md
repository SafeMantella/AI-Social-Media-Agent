# Pacing benchmark

Quantitative read of one long-form edit: **1,058 frames, 17 min 38 sec**. One video, one creator, one professional team. Use it as a market reference, not as a universal law. If this note is more than about 2–3 months old when you apply it, look for a newer pacing reference before trusting the counts.

**Standing rule:** a visual change at least every 2–3 seconds (cut, punch-in or zoom, b-roll, or a graphic in or out) is required. No static shot runs past 3 seconds unless it is a rare, deliberate heavy beat of 4–6 seconds.

**Captions do not count.** A cue change is text being read, not a cut, a reframe, or b-roll. An audit that counts cue changes reports false gaps of zero. Count only a real cut, a zoom while the ramp is moving (not during the hold), and an overlay entering or leaving.

## 1. Pacing counts

- **Visual events:** 552 in 1,058 seconds.
- **Mean interval:** one change every **1.92 seconds** (median 2.0s).
- **Shot length:** 1s **60.1%** (332), 2s **19.2%** (106), 3s **9.1%** (50), 4s or longer **11.6%** (64). The long shots are the heavy lines, not the default.
- **Three-second rule:** **88.4%** of visual changes happen in **3 seconds or less**.
- **Screen time:** talking shot **51.3%** (543s). B-roll and third-party video **23.6%** (250s). Cards, graphics, and split screens **10.6%** (112s). Transitions, glitches, and black holds **14.5%** (153s). Text or captions on screen **53.9%** (570s). Text on screen is not, by itself, a visual change.

## 2. Five jobs for b-roll

Each insert does one job. Generic stock that only "illustrates" does not.

1. **Historical, low-fi.** Scanlines, a CRT, a VHS texture. Anchor a new idea in an older change the viewer already understands. The source is often softer than the rest of the piece. Do not upscale it to hide that. The scanline or glitch is the style.
2. **Outside authority.** Real news or event footage, not a context-free stock clip. A lower-third with a 0.5s glitch names the source and the year. The clip lends credibility to the thesis. Use only footage the creator has the right to use.
2b. **Pop-culture joke.** A recognizable humor or meme clip. It does not lend credibility. Familiarity is the job. Keep it separate from authority.
3. **Live proof.** The real interface of the object the creator is describing, cursor and all. Speed 1.5×–2× and a slow push-in. It removes skepticism by showing the thing working. If the line names an on-screen action, show that screen within 5 seconds.
4. **Visual metaphor.** A concrete image for an abstract claim. Fast cuts, 0.8s–1.5s, on the attack of the spoken word.
5. **Branding and personal proof.** A card or a capture that anchors who is speaking. A pattern break around the two-minute mark.

## 3. Kinetic type

1. **Staccato.** One heavy sans-serif word per second, uppercase, centered. In on the phonetic attack, out on the silence. Use it for a spoken list of 3–5 items.
2. **Sentence stack.** The line builds in 3–4 cumulative lines on a dark textured card. It forces the definition to be read.
3. **Lower-third with a glitch.** Horizontal chromatic distortion for the first 12 frames, hold 2–3 seconds, leave on a hard cut.
4. **Chapter roadmap.** About five items, dim. At the start of a block, only the active item goes bright and bold.

## 4. One camera

- **Two crops from one 4K frame.** Medium-wide at 100% for the explanation. Punch-in at 130–140% for an alert, a shocking number, or the end of a point.
- **Jump cuts on a gesture.** Cut on a clap, the hands opening, or a point. The gesture hides a breath or a retake. The creator still makes the cut.

## 5. Archival footage

- Not every insert is a perfect metaphor. A professional edit still contains a generic shot. Do not stall looking for a perfect image.
- A cut inside real archival footage is marked (a fast-forward icon or a scrub texture), not hidden.
- Punch-ins (44 in the reference edit) outnumber glitch and scanline effects (18). Reframing the talking shot is the cheapest technique in the piece, and the one a solo edit can repeat. Archive categories 1, 2, 2b, and 4 need footage and a license. Do not copy them 1:1 without checking cost and rights. Categories 3 and 5 do not need a footage library.

## Audit

A cadence audit is part of finishing, not a one-off. Before a piece is ready to publish, count gaps longer than 3 seconds with no real visual change (captions excluded). Report the gaps. A punch-in or a b-roll insert happens only when the creator asks. The creator owns the cut. Re-audit the final assembly after any later pipeline change. A zoom pass that closed the gaps can be removed by a later version. Do not assume an earlier audit still holds.

## Checklist

- [ ] No talking shot sits longer than 3 seconds without a punch-in (about 130%), on-screen text entering, or a cut to b-roll. A 4–6s hold is rare and deliberate.
- [ ] Captions are excluded from the count. Zooms count only while the ramp moves.
- [ ] Live proof: if the line names an on-screen action, the real screen is in within 5 seconds.
- [ ] A spoken list of 3–5 items can use staccato words, phonetically locked.
- [ ] Outside clips are licensed, 3–5 seconds, with a source lower-third. If the license is unknown, do not insert them.
- [ ] A dim roadmap lights the active item at the start of a block.
- [ ] Archive b-roll is skipped when there is no license. Live proof and proof cards remain available.
