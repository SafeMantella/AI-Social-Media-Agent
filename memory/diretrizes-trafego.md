# Traffic

How attention moves. Copy is `estrategia/10-copywriting.md`. Ranking notes are `memory/growth_sales_critical_lens.md`. Do not copy those weights into this file.

Most traffic playbooks assume a catalog, a daily transaction, and a team. This system is one creator. Read the goal in `estrategia/01-posicionamento-e-pilares.md`. While that goal is growth or authority, traffic is almost all organic. Paid media tests an angle that already works. It does not cold-start an account. Spending to buy followers skips the asset.

Order, and do not skip a step:

1. **Native format, hook, and retention.** This is where the account grows.
2. **An owned destination.** A page, a list, or a lead magnet the creator controls. Every social profile is rented.
3. **Paid media.** After organic pieces have proved the angle, and after the destination converts.

Numbers below were collected on 2026-09-05 from platform docs and benchmark roundups. They decay. Re-check when this file is older than two or three months.

## 1. Organic

### What gets distributed

The ranking lens has the weights. The practical reading: the piece is the targeting. The first viewers teach the platform who else should see it. That is true on short video whether or not an ad account exists.

- Short video: watch time and replays, then sends, then likes. The first moments decide.
- A longer video on YouTube: the thumbnail, the title, and the first 30 seconds. Shorts follow the short-video logic.
- Search and a newsletter do not replace the short video. They catch the person the short video already interested. See `memory/content-format-cascade.md`.

### ABCD, for any short video

From Google's public creative framework. It fits a Reel or a Short.

- **Attract.** The first seconds. Tight frame, motion, a face when `face` is `yes`, tension or the result on screen. No intro card.
- **Brand.** The creator's voice or name shows up early, inside the piece. A static logo in the corner does not do this job.
- **Connect.** The viewer feels the problem. Describing it from outside is weaker.
- **Direct.** One clear next step.

### Creative metrics

Use the account's own average once it exists. Until then, these bands are directional:

- **Hook rate** = 3-second views / impressions. Weak under about 20%. Solid about 25–35%. Strong above about 35%. A weak number points at the first seconds.
- **Hold rate** = people still there after the open / 3-second views. Healthy above about 25–30%. A strong hook and a weak hold means the open over-promised. Cut the stall between the open and the proof.
- **Saves and sends** are the value signal. Likes are not.

Pull the retention curve. A drop at the start is the hook. A drop in the middle is the stall.

### Cadence and shape

- Cold-start floor: **3–4 short videos and 1–2 carousels a week**, plus a story when there is a real moment. `estrategia/09-sprint-cold-start-30-dias.md`.
- **Rewrite the caption and the frame per platform.** The file can be shared. The words around it are native.
- **Document the real work.** The bench, the bug, the delivery, the opinion. That removes "I have nothing to post" without inventing a series. A light recurring series is optional and comes through the feedback loop, not from this file.
- **One dense piece becomes several native ones.** Mark the atoms first. Each derivative needs its own angle.

### What makes someone send it

Six pressures, from Jonah Berger's public framework. Use them as a check, not as a quota.

- **Social currency.** The sender looks informed. A surprising specific, an insider step.
- **Triggers.** Tie the idea to a moment that already recurs in this niche (a launch, a bill, a Monday task). The moment comes from the audience in `estrategia/01`.
- **High-arousal emotion.** Awe or a real warning. A mild take does not travel.
- **Practical value.** Something they can do today.
- **A story that cannot drop the method.** If the story still works after the creator's method is removed, the send does not carry the brand.
- The honest metric is **sends plus saves**.

## 2. When paid turns on

Spend nothing until all three exist:

1. At least 3–5 organic pieces that beat this account's average on hook rate and on saves or sends.
2. A destination whose first screen repeats the promise of the piece, with one conversion event (a signup, a download, a booking).
3. A low-friction offer for a cold viewer: the lead magnet, a guide, a short quiz, the list. A cold viewer does not land on a sales page.

Leave these to catalog businesses. They do not belong on a personal brand with no catalog:

- Shopping feeds, catalog ads, dynamic product ads.
- Value-based bidding and a target return on ad spend, before there is a stream of transactions.
- Many ad sets split by tiny interests. A small budget then never leaves learning.
- A multi-touch attribution suite. Use campaign parameters on every link, plus whatever simple path the destination already logs. Last touch is enough.
- Bought followers. Inactive followers train the account toward people who will not watch.

## 3. The smallest ad account

- **One or two campaigns.** One prospecting (broad, or the platform's automated broad delivery). One retargeting (viewed the video, visited the page, or is on the list).
- **Budget at campaign level.** Let delivery move money.
- **A broad audience.** The piece selects who stays. A tight interest stack slows learning.
- **About 80% prospecting and 20% retargeting.** Do not exhaust the people who already know the creator.
- Data quality means the conversion event is real and server-side where the platform allows it. A browser-only signal under-counts.

## 4. What the creator actually controls in the auction

Rank is a bid times an estimated action rate times quality. The practical lever is not a larger budget. It is a higher click-through and a page that matches the promise. A slow page, or a page that opens on a different claim, raises the cost.

## 5. Bidding

- Start on **maximize conversions**, with no target cost, until the account has history. Move to a target cost around **30 conversions in a month** on that campaign.
- **Learning is fragile.** After a create or a large edit, leave budget, pieces, and bid alone for about **7 days**, or until roughly **30–50 conversions**. Daily tinkering restarts learning.
- Structure each campaign so it can reach that volume. Below it, merge campaigns. Do not add another one.

## 6. Measurement that is enough

- The conversion event is the bottom of this funnel: the list signup, the lead-magnet download, or the purchase click. That is what delivery learns from.
- Put tracking parameters on every link. Compare the platform's number with the destination's number. Platforms claim credit for each other.
- A common window is 7 days after a click and 1 day after a view. Say which window you used.
- Do not call a winner on a handful of conversions. Wait until the volume in the learning note above.
- Test one structural change with a real split. Test a headline for free on organic first.

## 7. Temperature

Read this against the funnel in `estrategia/07-funil-de-conteudo.md`.

| Temperature | Who they are | Angle | Destination |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold | Does not know the creator. May not have named the problem. | A pattern break, the symptom, a common myth, a result they can see | Short video, a teaching piece, the lead magnet |
| Warm | Knows solutions exist. Does not yet trust this creator. | A demonstration, a comparison, a real case, the work in public | A deeper piece, the list |
| Hot | Knows the creator and has a reason to act | The offer, the deadline, the proof, the bonus | The checkout, the booking, the launch |

Cold traffic buys an understanding of the problem. Hot traffic can be asked for the transaction. A cold viewer who meets a hot pitch leaves.

Give away several useful pieces before the ask. One call to action. It has to work on a phone with no extra steps.

The social post attracts. The list is where a later offer can be made more than once. Organic reach of a post falls off. The list does not.

## 8. Benchmarks (directional, 2026-09-05, re-check)

Roundups, not this account's numbers:

- A healthy link click-through on short-video ads is often above about 1–1.5%. Search click-through is often several times higher. Display is often under 1%.
- Frequency above about 2.5–3.5 on cold traffic inside 7 days means the piece or the audience is tired.
- Hook rate above about 30% and hold rate above about 25% is "validated" only against these bands. Prefer the account's own average once a month of data exists.
- A break-even return is 1 divided by contribution margin. Ignore it until there is a paid offer. Until then the number that matters is cost per lead and whether the list is growing.

## 9. Decision checklist

1. Is the goal still audience growth? Then organic, native format, hook. Paid is an angle test.
2. Do 3–5 organic pieces already beat this account? If not, do not boost.
3. Does the destination match the promise and fire one conversion event? If not, build that first.
4. Is the account one or two campaigns, broad, budget on the campaign? More than that gets merged.
5. Can the campaign see about 30–50 conversions in a month? If not, it will sit in learning. Change the offer or the budget. Do not add campaigns.
6. Is cold traffic being talked to as cold? A cold click into a sales page is a miss.
7. Will the account sit untouched for about 7 days after launch? A plan that "optimizes daily" gets cut.

## 10. What to keep from older playbooks

Older books describe dead networks and bought followers. Keep the moves that still transfer:

- **Change one variable, read the result, then pivot.**
- **Give value several times, then make one ask.** Native to each platform. The ask has no extra friction.
- **Show the real work** instead of inventing a topic.
- **Rented reach versus an owned list.**
- **The six send-pressures** in section 1.

Drop shopping structure, follower arbitrage, and any rule that says most posts should be links outward. Outbound links are a tax on reach. Put the depth on the owned page and point to it on purpose.

## 11. What modern ad teams do, and what a solo creator keeps

Large teams consolidate to a few campaigns, let automated delivery pick the audience, and treat the creative as the targeting. That last point already matches organic short video. Use it now.

Keep:

- **Concept versus iteration.** Fatigue hits the hook first. An iteration changes the hook, the first frame, or the ask. A new concept changes the idea. Make a new concept when iterations stop moving the metric.
- **A short brief before a shoot:** who it is for, the pain in their words, the one hypothesis, what must be on screen, and a reference for the structure (not for the visual skin).
- **Visual variety with the same person** when `face` is `yes`. The face can stay. The setting and the proof should change. When `face` is `no`, vary the proof on screen instead. Do not add cutaways whose job is to hide a face.
- **Read a competitor's public ads to find the gap**, then take the other side. Do not clone the skin. The visual system in `estrategia/00-identidade-visual.md` stays.

Leave for a catalog team: a dozen to twenty new ad variants a week, media-mix models, and a blended-return stack. A small account with one or two channels reads the ads manager against leads and against the destination's own count. That is enough.

When revenue exists, three extra numbers earn a place: total revenue over total ad spend, new-customer revenue over total ad spend, and contribution margin (sales minus ad spend minus the direct cost of delivering). They do not replace the organic metrics while the goal is growth.
