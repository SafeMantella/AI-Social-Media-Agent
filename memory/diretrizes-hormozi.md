# Offer, acquisition, money model

This is the business layer under copy and traffic. It is not a second copy guide.

Order of the three books, as a mental model: what is worth buying, who can be reached, and how the offers are sequenced so growth does not need outside cash. The notes below are the operational extract. They are rules of thumb from those books, not this account's results.

Read `Goal` in `estrategia/01-posicionamento-e-pilares.md`. Section 5 runs now. Section 3 stays parked until a paid offer exists.

## 1. The offer

### Value equation

Use it on every piece, not only on a price.

```
Value = (dream outcome × felt odds of getting it)
        ─────────────────────────────────────────
        (time to the result × effort and sacrifice)
```

Raise the outcome and the felt odds. Lower the time and the effort.

Felt odds go up when the creator shows a real result: the screen, the photo, the number from their own work. Time and effort go down when the viewer leaves with a checklist, a template, or steps they can run today. The dream outcome is the promise already written in `estrategia/01`. Do not invent a bigger one in the caption.

### Building the offer

1. Write the dream outcome in the audience's words.
2. List every obstacle between them and that outcome. Run each obstacle through the four parts of the equation.
3. Turn each obstacle into "how to get past this, fast."
4. List many ways to deliver each solution: a conversation, a group, done-for-you, a tool, a recorded lesson, a checklist. The point is to see more than one.
5. Cut high cost and low value. Keep low cost and high value (a checklist, a template, a recorded lesson) and the few high-cost items that are worth it. Stack what remains.

### Naming

A name can test an angle without reshooting the lesson. Five slots:

- **Magnet.** Why now.
- **Avatar.** Who it is for, from the audience in `estrategia/01`.
- **Goal.** The result.
- **Interval.** How long it takes.
- **Container.** The wrapper word: challenge, blueprint, guide, playbook.

Fill the slots from the brand files. A name that could sit on any creator's piece gets rewritten.

### Guarantees

Only after a paid offer has been sold and the refund behavior is known.

- Unconditional: the money comes back.
- Conditional: the money comes back if they did the work.
- Anti-guarantee: the sale is final, for something that is consumed immediately.
- Performance: paid from the result.

Do not print a guarantee on a free piece.

## 2. Acquisition

### Engaged lead

A lead is someone the creator can contact. An engaged lead has shown interest in the thing being offered. The growth bottleneck is the rate of new engaged leads, not a vanity follower count.

### Seven steps for a lead magnet

1. Which narrow problem, and for whom.
2. How it is solved.
3. How it is delivered (a file, a tool, a service).
4. Test the name.
5. Easy to consume.
6. Good enough to charge for.
7. One clear next step.

The magnet solves one complete slice. It reveals the next problem, which the paid offer solves later. While nothing is for sale, the "payment" is a way to reach them again, plus the reply. Mold: `freebies/README.md`. The piece has to be allowed by the "never do" section of `estrategia/01`.

### Four ways to get a lead

| | One to one | One to many |
|---|---|---|
| Warm (they know the creator) | A personal note to someone who already replied | Publishing free pieces. This is the default. |
| Cold (they do not) | Cold outreach | Paid distribution |

Cold outreach and paid distribution wait until the publishing path is producing engaged leads, or until `Goal` is sales and the traffic prerequisites in `memory/diretrizes-trafego.md` are true.

### More, then better, then new

In that order.

1. **More.** Double the input: more pieces, more real conversations.
2. **Better.** Find where people drop (hook, hold, the destination, the reply) and fix that conversion.
3. **New.** A new format, then a new platform, then a new box in the table above. Only after more and better are exhausted.

### A hundred a day

One hundred primary actions a day, for one hundred days. While the goal is growth or authority, the action is **one hundred minutes a day making and publishing**. That matches the cold-start floor in `estrategia/09-sprint-cold-start-30-dias.md`.

### Handing acquisition to someone else

Customers who refer, then employees, then an agency, then affiliates. Do not hand the Core Four to a third party until the creator has done it and the margin can pay for the help.

### Rules of thumb he publishes

- Lifetime value to acquisition cost at or above **3 to 1**, once there is a price.
- Cold email: about **3%** of the list becoming an engaged lead is the bar he cites (open rate times reply rate). It is a target to test, not a promise.
- The top slice of advertisers tests many more pieces than the median. On this system that shows up as hook iterations, not as a team of editors.
- A very high historical return he cites comes from a high-ticket service and a huge audience. It is not a target for this account.

## 3. Money model (parked until a paid offer exists)

Four offers in a sequence:

1. **Attraction.** Turns a stranger into a buyer and covers the cost to acquire them.
2. **Upsell.** Solves the next problem right after the yes.
3. **Downsell.** A smaller yes after a no: a payment plan, a trial, or fewer features at a lower price.
4. **Continuity.** A recurring reason to stay, which raises lifetime value.

Client-financed acquisition means the gross profit from a customer inside **30 days** is larger than the cost to acquire and serve them. The business can grow without borrowing.

Two numbers to keep parked with the rest: a premium anchor several times the main price, and a "win your money back" offer only when refunds are already rare. Do not design these in a caption while nothing is for sale.

## 4. How the three fit

The offer says what is worth buying. Acquisition says who can be reached. The money model says how the first 30 days pay for the next customer. While there is no price, the creator is building the chassis: the audience, the proof, and the list. The engine waits.

## 5. What to run now

1. **Publish.** One hundred minutes a day while the goal is growth or authority.
2. **A lead magnet as soon as the goal is leads or sales,** and as soon as growth has a real way to send people back. High value, cheap to duplicate. The exchange is a contact. It has to survive the "never do" list.
3. **The value equation on every piece.** Proof up. Time and effort down.
4. **Name pieces, series, and free guides** with the five slots. Test the angle in the name.
5. **Write by hand to the first people who reply.** That is voice-of-customer research (`estrategia/10-copywriting.md`) and it matches the two-way signal in `memory/growth_sales_critical_lens.md`.
6. **More, then better, then new** on content. Volume, then the hook, then a new platform.
7. **A sequence of attention** with nothing for sale yet: the free piece, then the list, then the place they can keep following.

## 6. What stays parked

- Buying attention to feed a page that does not convert.
- A 30-day payback model, guarantees, upsells, downsells, anchors, continuity pricing.
- Affiliates and agencies.

Turn section 3 and this list on only when `estrategia/01` says the goal is sales, or when a paid offer is actually live. Say which of those is true before drafting the sequence.
