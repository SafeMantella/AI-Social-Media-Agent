# Copywriting

The method for any hook, script, carousel, caption, or long post. Precedence when two files disagree is `memory/diretrizes-copywriting.md`. Read that table first.

Examples in this guide are slots. Fill them from the brand files, which are blank until onboarding:

- Niche, audience, promise, goal, pillars, never-do: `estrategia/01-posicionamento-e-pilares.md`
- Voice: `memory/estilo-de-escrita/guia-voz.md`
- Openers the creator chose to reuse: `referencia/swipe-openers.md`
- Proof: a real file the creator supplies

Do not invent a niche to demonstrate a formula.

## 1. Principles that outlast a ranking change

**Specific beats vague.** A precise claim is believed. A puff word ("powerful", "revolutionary") is skipped. Replace it with a number, an object, or a scene from the creator's own work.

**The line is a salesperson, not a mood.** Each sentence has to move the reader toward the action you chose before writing. If it does not, cut it.

**Enter the conversation already in their head.** The audience in `estrategia/01` is already worried about something. Name that. Do not open by introducing the creator.

**People act on feeling and justify with reason.** The feeling is the pain or the status in the promise. The reason is the proof on screen.

**The title selects.** A hook that tries to please everyone reaches no one. It should be obvious who it is for, using the audience description.

**The first line's only job is to earn the second line.** Then the next. A piece that is easy to keep reading beats a piece that is impressive and stops.

**Write short words and short sentences.** Read it aloud. Where you stumble, the viewer leaves.

**Show the result.** A sample they can use (a checklist, a template, the steps in the caption) lowers the risk of trusting a stranger.

**Test two hooks.** Retention and the click are the measurement. Do not ship the first line you liked.

**Write the action before the draft.** If you cannot say what the viewer should do at the end, the piece is not ready.

## 2. Before the draft

### Voice of customer

The words come from the audience, not from a blank page.

Where to look, using only the tools `memory/config.md` enabled: public comments, replies, reviews, threads in the communities this niche actually uses. Record a gap when a tool is off. Do not pretend you read it.

Four questions for each quote:

1. What was happening in their life before they looked for a fix?
2. What almost stopped them?
3. What was the moment it worked, including a benefit they did not expect?
4. What did they say, verbatim?

Keep a sheet with columns: source, pain, desire, objection, exact words. When the cursor blinks, copy the exact-words column into the hook and edit only what the voice file requires. A polished paraphrase ("we optimize your workflow") loses to the sentence they actually used.

A trigger event is the day the problem became urgent. Ask the research for those days. The piece that opens on the trigger beats the piece that opens on the category.

### Five levels of awareness

Pick the level before the hook. The level decides the lead.

1. **Unaware.** They have not named the problem. Open with a story or an identity they recognize. Do not name the offer.
2. **Problem aware.** They know the pain and not the way out. This is most cold traffic. Open on the pain in their words.
3. **Solution aware.** They know the kind of fix and not which path. Open on the difference between paths.
4. **Product aware.** They know this creator. They need proof and a reason to act now.
5. **Most aware.** They need the link and the single ask.

Selling at level 5 to a level 2 viewer is the cold-versus-hot mistake in `memory/diretrizes-trafego.md`.

### Market sophistication

If the niche has heard the same promise for years, a louder version of it fails. Name the mechanism: the specific way this creator gets the result. The promise in `estrategia/01` should already be that specific. Sell the how, not the category.

## 3. The hook

Most of the result is the first line. Treat the writing time accordingly.

Four tests for a finished hook:

- **Useful.** A practical problem moves.
- **Urgent.** A change, a risk, or a deadline they already feel.
- **Unique.** An angle that is not the category slogan.
- **Specific.** A number, an object, or a situation from their life.

A curiosity gap gives the clue and withholds the trick. A definition that finishes the thought in the same sentence closes the gap and the viewer leaves. One short sentence. The jargon rule in `memory/feedback_hook_writing_no_jargon.md` still wins.

### Formulas

Fill the brackets from `estrategia/01` and from the voice-of-customer sheet. If the filled line could be any creator in any niche, rewrite it.

| Formula | Shape |
|---|---|
| Benefit, time, objection | Get [result] in [time], even if [objection they actually say]. |
| How to | How to [do the thing] so that [result they want]. |
| Reason why | [Number] reasons [specific situation] keeps happening. |
| Secret of a group | What [the audience] does to get [result] without [the painful default]. |

### Four families

Power openers are starting shapes. The swipe file holds real ones from this niche. Rewrite them.

| Family | Starting shape |
|---|---|
| Curiosity | "There is a step nobody mentions…" / "The worst advice about [topic]…" |
| Pain | "You spend [time] on [task]…" / "Stop [the thing they already regret]…" |
| Promise | "The exact way to [result]…" / "How to [result] in [time]…" |
| Story | "Last [time], I [specific mistake]…" / "The worst [object] I trusted…" |

Write at least three, in different families, before the body. The script pipeline already requires this. Add one line on why each hook should work.

## 4. Frameworks, and when

| Framework | Shape | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| AIDA | Attention, interest, desire, action | The default short video and a fast post |
| PAS | Problem, agitate, solution | A sharp pain, a carousel, a longer short video |
| BAB | Before, after, bridge | A real case, a progress note |
| 4 Ps | Promise, picture, proof, push | A dense caption, a launch of something free or paid |
| 4 Cs | Clear, concise, compelling, credible | Editing. A pass, not a plot |
| One-liner | Person, problem, plan, success | The bio and a 30-second spoken introduction |
| FAB | Feature, advantage, benefit | A tool, a product, a setup. End on the benefit to them |

The one-liner is filled from `estrategia/01`: who the audience is, the pain, the plan this creator actually uses, the result in the promise.

4 Cs fails the piece if it is muddy, long, dull, or unproved. Fix the failed C. Do not add adjectives.

## 5. How a piece is built

### Leads, from most aware to least

Use the awareness level from section 2. The matching lead:

1. **Offer.** The thing and the ask. Most-aware only.
2. **Promise.** The result, stated plainly.
3. **Problem-solution.** The wound, then the fix.
4. **Secret.** A mechanism other people in the niche are not saying.
5. **Proclamation.** A claim that is specific enough to be checked.
6. **Story.** A scene. The mechanism has to be inside the scene, or the story is only entertainment.
7. **Prediction.** A dated change and what it does to this audience. A prediction with no date is a slogan.

### Lines that pull the next line

Use sparingly, in the creator's voice, not as a stack of catchphrases. Useful jobs: "Here is the part that hurts." "The result:" "Which means:" "It sounds harder than it is." If the voice file bans a phrase, the voice file wins.

### Short video

The creator cuts. The agent writes the script and, after `base.mp4` returns, the overlays and the captions.

- **Open.** One sentence on screen. A moving image of the problem or the result. No intro.
- **Next beats.** The pain, then the mechanism, then the proof on screen. Proof is a file, not a sentence that says "trust me".
- **Close.** One ask.
- **On-screen captions** follow the video pipeline: at most 5 words a cue, last in the edit.

### Carousel

Build rules: `estrategia/03-templates-sops-calendario.md`. Slide 1 earns the swipe. Slide 2 pays it off with something concrete. Middle slides teach one idea each. The last slide is one ask. The post caption is 1 to 3 sentences. The slides already carry the lesson.

### Post caption under a short video

The first 125 characters work with the rest cut off. They complement the video. They do not repeat the spoken hook word for word and then stop. The body can hold the steps, the definition, and the link, in short paragraphs. One ask at the end. Voice rules still apply.

## 6. Clarity

Edit in this order:

1. Write the messy draft.
2. Read it as a skeptical viewer interrupting every sentence. Answer the interruption or cut the sentence.
3. Read it aloud. Shorten the breath you cannot finish.
4. Cut the warm-up paragraph. The piece starts at the first line that would still make sense if it were the hook.
5. Prefer the active verb. Prefer a number or an object to an adverb.
6. Put the important words at the start of the line. Most people scan.

A contrast helps when it is a real one from the niche: the old way, the new way, both specific. A string of "not this, but that" is a tic. One in a piece is enough. The vetoes section covers the rest.

## 7. Writing like a creator

**One idea, about 250–300 words when the format is a short essay.** Headline with the four tests. First line on the pain. Two lines that show you know the situation. Then the steps. A close that asks for one action or states the implication. It does not restate the headline.

**One idea, several formats.** Do not hunt a new topic every day. From the same idea, a how-to, a contrary take, a common mistake, a scene from the work, or a list. Each one needs its own angle (`memory/content-format-cascade.md`).

**The position is an intersection.** The pillars in `estrategia/01` are that intersection. A piece that only one of those pillars could have produced is on strategy. A piece any account in the category could post is not.

## 8. Email and the list

Write this section only when the goal is leads or sales, or when a list already exists. While the goal is growth or authority and nothing is being sent, skip it.

- Send often enough that a person remembers the voice. A monthly corporate letter loses to a shorter note tied to something that happened in the work.
- Most of the note is a scene or a useful step. The ask, when it exists, is at the end.
- A five-note welcome, if you use one: set the scene and do not finish it; the worst moment; the discovery; the benefit they did not expect; one ask. Each note except the last opens the next. Do not fake a disaster the creator did not live.

## 9. Why they stay

They did not come "for content". They hired the piece to get the outcome in the promise: status, relief, a skill, a saved hour. Write to that job.

Open on the trigger event from the voice-of-customer sheet when you have one.

The one-sentence persuasion check: the piece should encourage a desire they already have, justify a failure that was not stupidity, calm a fear, confirm a suspicion they can verify, or point at a shared opponent. The shared opponent has to be allowed by the never-do section. A vague industry is not an opponent. A specific bad practice is.

## 10. Proof and belonging

Showing the work is the authority. A paragraph that claims authority without a file is weaker than a quiet screen recording.

Write as a peer of the audience in `estrategia/01`. The voice file says whether the creator is a guide, a peer, or something else. Follow the voice file.

Scarcity and a hard close wait for a real offer (`memory/diretrizes-hormozi.md`).

## 11. Format cheat sheet

| Format | Open | Body | Close |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short video | One glance, sound off, one sentence | Proof arrives before the viewer wonders if this is empty | One micro ask |
| Carousel | Slide 1 does the work. Slide 2 confirms value | One idea a slide. Obvious beats clever | One ask. Caption is 1–3 sentences |
| Video caption | First 125 characters stand alone | Steps, definition, link. Short lines | One ask |
| Long post or email | The scene or the pain | The mechanism and the proof | One implication or one ask |

## 12. Patterns worth stealing

Steal the structure. Do not steal the wording of a famous ad, and do not steal the skin of a viral video. Fill every pattern from this niche.

1. **Two people, same start.** Same situation, two outcomes. The difference is the mechanism, and the mechanism is inseparable from the story.
2. **They doubted, then they saw.** A specific disbelief, then the proof on screen.
3. **The mistake you do not know you make.** Name the behavior from the voice-of-customer sheet.
4. **The easier path that is still true.** Leverage, not a lie. If the path is not easier, do not claim it.
5. **The field is lying about one point.** A specific false belief, then the evidence. Not "everything you know is wrong".
6. **Identity.** "If you are the kind of person who [behavior from the audience]…" The behavior has to be real.
7. **A startling first frame.** The moving image is the pattern interrupt. It still has to match the sentence. A shock with no promise is a trick.
8. **A commodity explained by its mechanism.** Do not say the category. Say the step that makes this version different.
9. **Relief of a pain they can point at.** The pain is a scene, not the word "struggle".
10. **An unlikely specific.** A concrete object, age, or constraint that makes the result surprising, then the reason.

## 13. What changed, and what did not

What changed: this audience can check a claim. Proof on screen outweighs a bigger adjective. The first glance is shorter than a paragraph.

What did not: self-interest, specificity, and entering a conversation that is already happening. A hungry situation still beats a clever line. Find the situation in the research. Do not invent one.

## 14. Checklist before approval

1. Does the first line talk about their situation, or about the creator's credentials?
2. Can every puff word be swapped for a number, an object, or a file?
3. Does the hook survive "so what?", asked in the audience's voice?
4. Can the first paragraph be cut without a loss? If yes, cut it.
5. Does each line pull the next?
6. Is there proof on screen, or only a claim?
7. Is there one ask, and is it possible on a phone?
8. Are the key phrases theirs, from the sheet?
9. Does the voice file still recognize the piece?
10. Is the mechanism named, so the piece is not a generic tutorial?
11. Does the piece pass the vetoes below?
12. Read aloud. Fix the stumble.

The pre-publish rubric in `memory/checklist-revisao-pre-publicacao.md` still applies. A score under the minimum does not ship.

## 15. Slots, not samples

Replace every bracket. Do not publish a bracket. Do not drop in a niche the brand files do not contain.

**Carousel, teaching**

- Slide 1: You keep losing [time or money] to [the exact pain], trying to [the task they already attempt].
- Slide 2: The stuck point is [the old method], not [the thing they blame].
- Slide 3: Three steps to [result in the promise].
- Last slide: One ask. Save, send, or the comment word already stored in `memory/dm-automation.md` when DM automation is on.

**Short video**

- 0–2s, on screen: [one sentence, one family, no unexplained jargon].
- Next: why the default fails, in their words.
- Then: the mechanism, with the proof file visible.
- Close: where the steps live (caption or list), and one ask.

**Caption or note, story lead**

Open on a scene the creator lived. One turn. The mechanism in two or three sentences. What it changes for the reader. One link or one ask. If the scene did not happen, do not write it.

## 16. Vetoes

Reject the line. Do not negotiate with it.

**Opens and closes that announce the piece instead of starting it:** "today we will talk about", "in this post you will learn", "before we start", "have you ever wondered", "in a world where", "many people ask". Closes: "hope you liked it", "thanks for watching", "if this helped", "at the end of the day", "the question is".

**Titles that could be anyone's:** "when X becomes Y", "the rise of X", "the impact of X", "everything you need to know about X", "the ultimate guide to X", "discover how", "X has changed forever".

**Tics:** "and that changes everything", "simply", "basically", "more and more", "it is no accident", "the truth is". A forced contrast used as the skeleton of the piece ("it is not X, it is Y" stacked, "less X, more Y" stacked). One contrast in a piece, if the voice file allows it.

**Brochure words:** ecosystem, synergy, disruptive, game-changer, revolutionary, "only one on the market", "think outside the box", and "innovative" with nothing specific beside it.

**A number with no source.** "Studies show", "experts say", "most people", "recently". Replace with the figure, the source, and the year, or cut it.

**Headlines with no picture and no proof.** If nobody knows what to put on screen, the line is not done. If the line still works after you swap in another creator's name from a different niche, it is generic. Rewrite until it only fits the promise in `estrategia/01`.

**Six tests for an important piece:**

1. **Swap.** Replace the creator with a peer. If the line still fits, rewrite.
2. **A real person.** Would someone in the audience say this or send it? An internal announcement fails.
3. **Mechanism.** Does it imply a tool, a step, or a result? An abstract benefit fails.
4. **Visual.** Can you point at the frame? If not, rewrite.
5. **Proof.** Is there a file behind the sentence?
6. **Platform.** Can it become a short video, a cover, a caption, and a carousel without a patch on each?

**Script anti-patterns:** do not open with "did you know". Do not open with "in this video I will show you". Do not end with no ask. Do not paste one caption to every platform. Do not slice one piece into many posts without a new angle. Do not publish the derivatives on the same day. Read aloud and ask whether a hurried viewer would still be here.

**A modern hook shape that earns its place:** a dated fact, then the obvious cause denied, then the real cause. The fact needs a source. A rhetorical question as the open is the weaker habit.

**The close pivots.** It names what changes for the viewer. It does not summarize the piece they just finished.

**Cutting beats adding.** Several passes. Each sentence has to do a job.

## 17. Where the ideas come from

The private reading notes behind the original method do not ship in this template. The principles are public and old enough to paraphrase:

- Claude Hopkins, *Scientific Advertising* (specificity, testing, the reader as the subject).
- Robert Collier (enter the existing conversation).
- Gary Halbert (the first line earns the next, a hungry situation, short words).
- Eugene Schwartz (awareness and sophistication), as commonly taught. The book itself is not reproduced here.
- Public writing notes from Julian Shapiro, Harry Dry, Copyhackers (voice-of-customer mining), and Nielsen Norman Group (people scan).
- Creator-writing habits: one idea at a time, one idea in several formats, a position that is an intersection.
- Offer and list mechanics stay in `memory/diretrizes-hormozi.md`. Distribution stays in `memory/diretrizes-trafego.md`. The weekly playbook is `memory/estrategias-principais.md`.

When a source's number matters to a decision, re-check it. Do not treat this file as a substitute for that check.
